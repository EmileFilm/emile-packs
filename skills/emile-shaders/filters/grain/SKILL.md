# Grain

Takes the digital shine off: the picture gets a surface, like film or a good print. A new grain every frame, strongest in the mid-tones. A little goes a long way.

## Role
Finish: the last filter on top, over colour and structure.

## When to use
Only when the picture is too clean for what it is saying.
- **People and feeling** (a founder's story, a testimonial, a quiet moment): amount 0.2–0.3, size 1.5.
- **Night and mood** (dark product shots, a tense build-up): amount 0.3–0.45, size 2.
- **One film from many sources** (screen recordings, footage and drawn clips together): the whole film at 0.15–0.25 so they share a surface.
- Words that call for it: grain, film grain, 16 mm, texture, analogue, organic, cinematic.

## Suits
- Every kind of picture checked (products by day and by night, portraits, a landscape): the picture is never lost under it.
- Flat, drawn or rendered clips that look too smooth.

## Doesn't suit
- Small text and fine interface at amount above 0.3: the grain sits in the letters.
- Footage that is already noisy.
- A bright, crisp, high-tech look.

## The tired version
The tired version is television static at full strength, or "cinematic" grain on everything. For a whole film stay at 0.3 or under; size 1–2; coloured grain only for a deliberately rough look.

## How long
A finish, so it may run long: at amount 0.25 or under it can cover the whole film and give footage from different sources one surface. Heavier (0.4 and up) only for one moody section.

## Parameters
- `amount` 0–1: 0.15–0.3 a touch, 0.35 the default, 0.4–0.7 an obvious film look.
- `size` 0.5–6 (pixels of a 1080-pixel frame): 1–2 fine, 3 and up coarse.
- `colour`: grain in colour instead of grey.
