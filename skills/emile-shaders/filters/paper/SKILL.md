# Paper

Makes the film an object instead of a screen: the lights take the sheet's tone, ink never quite reaches black, and a tooth lit from one side shows under everything. Nothing moves.

## Role
Finish: on top, over colour. Not with halftone, risograph or cmyk: they bring their own paper.

## When to use
Only when the film should feel printed, written or drawn.
- **A storybook explainer** (drawn clips, illustration, flat colour): the whole film, texture 0.4–0.6.
- **Words on a page** (a letter, a manifesto, values, a quote): texture 0.5, fibres 0.4.
- **Heritage and craft** (a maker's brand, stationery, food, books).
- Words that call for it: paper, sheet, letterpress, watercolour paper, tactile, matte, storybook, stationery.

## Suits
- Light pages, illustrations and flat graphics: the tooth shows best on light, even areas.
- Type of any size: the texture doesn't break letters.

## Doesn't suit
- Dark scenes: the sheet can't be seen under dark ink, and the blacks only go grey.
- Photographs and footage meant to look real: the tooth reads as dirt on the lens.

## The tired version
The tired version is yellowed parchment with stains and burnt edges. Keep the tone close to white (#f1e8d6 warm, #eef0ec cool), texture at 0.6 or under.

## How long
A finish, so it may run long: an illustrated explainer or words on a page can take it for a section or the whole film (texture 0.5 or under). Never on live footage or product shots.

## Parameters
- `amount` 0–1: 1 is the print on the sheet.
- `texture` 0–1: the tooth and the mottle; 0.5 the default.
- `fibres` 0–1: the long fibres in the sheet.
- `tone`: the paper's colour.
