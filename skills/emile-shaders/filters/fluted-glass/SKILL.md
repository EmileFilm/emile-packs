# Fluted glass

Puts a pane of reeded glass in front of the picture: you can tell what is behind it, not read it. Each rib squeezes a strip of the picture, with a seam of shadow and a line of light.

## Role
Optics: over the picture and its colour, under a finish. A filter clip changes every track under it, so type that must be read goes on a track above the clip.

## When to use
Only when something should be sensed before it is seen, or kept private.
- **Before the reveal** (the product or its screen behind glass for the opening seconds, then a cut to the clear picture).
- **A ground for a headline** (footage, a gradient or a portrait behind glass, the words above the filter clip, sharp).
- **Privacy and trust** (security, confidential data, "what others can't see").
- Words that call for it: fluted glass, reeded glass, ribbed glass, frosted, behind glass, refraction, privacy.

## Suits
- Portraits: a face stays a face behind the ribs (checked on three portraits).
- Products, gradients and colourful footage; shapes wider than about three ribs.
- amount under 1 is thinner glass (less bend and frost), not a mix of two pictures.

## Doesn't suit
- Anything that must be read: letters double behind the ribs (a product's screen turns unreadable). Keep the words on a track above.
- Thin lines and small interface: they break up.

## The tired version
The tired version is a weak wavy wobble like a shower door, or a rainbow "glass" sticker. Keep refraction at 0.4 or more so it reads as glass, width 0.03–0.06, and the ribs upright (angle 0) or level (90).

## How long
An accent: 1–3 seconds before a reveal, or a ground for one line of large type. Never over information that must be read; words go on a track above the filter.

## Parameters
- `amount` 0–1: how much glass.
- `width` 0.01–0.2 (share of the frame's short side): 0.02 fine reeds, 0.04 the default, 0.08 wide flutes.
- `refraction` 0–1: how hard each rib squeezes the picture.
- `frost` 0–1: softness across the ribs.
- `angle` 0–180: 0 upright ribs, 90 level ones.
