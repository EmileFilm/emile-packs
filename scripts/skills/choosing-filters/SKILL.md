# Choosing a filter

One page over the fifteen free filters, drawn from each pack's own guide: what each is for, how long it holds up, and where it fails. Choose here, then read the chosen pack's guide in full (`read_pack`) before placing or tuning it: its values are the ones to use.

## How to choose

1. **Start from the moment, not the filter.** Find the moment in the film that calls for a look (a headline that should feel published, a memory, the problem before the product, a hit on the beat) and the filter whose moments below match it. No moment calls for one: use none.
2. **Keep to its length.** Most filters are an accent of a few seconds over the moment that calls for them, between clean shots. With no span named, lay it over that moment only, as long as its How long says, and say where you put it. Only a light finish may run the whole film unasked.
3. **Check the picture against Fails on.** A dark shot, an interface, numbers or small type under a strong filter: use the guide's mending setting, move the words to a track above the filter clip, or choose another filter.
4. **Stack by role.** Colour goes lowest, a medium (`crt`, `vhs`) just under the finish, a finish (`grain`, `paper`) on top; each Role below says where it goes and what doesn't go with it (`paper` never with a print filter, nothing over `dither`).

## By kind of film

Where each filter belongs in each kind of film: **long** may run a section or the whole film, **moment** only the seconds that call for it, **avoid** fails on that kind of picture. A filter not named is not ruled out, only not a usual choice. Settings in brackets are the ones checked; without them, the guide's defaults.

Checked on 2026-11-16 by drawing two photographs of each kind (58 in all) under every filter with Émile's compositor: how much of the run from dark to light is left, whether the picture can still be found in it, how far its colours move, and by eye for small text, which the measures miss.

What the check found beyond the guides:
- bloom 1.0.0 did almost nothing on real photographs; bloom 2.0.0, drawn in passes, glows on 33 of the 58 (every dark one with points of light) and leaves the light ones unchanged. A white screen in a daylight room glows like a lamp: use it there only for a dream.
- edge-glow (new, drawn in passes) finds outlines in the picture, so it lights clear silhouettes well and fails on close faces (lines across the cheeks) and crowded pictures (broken pieces); it was drawn on the same 58 photographs.
- On a dark picture, halftone and palette need exposure 0.5–0.8 (as their guides say); at 0 the subject disappears.
- risograph on a picture of one hue keeps under half of the run from dark to light.
- grain at 0.2 and chromatic-aberration at strength 0.25 are hard to see at a glance: that is what lets them run long. Judge them at full size.

## Looks made of several filters

Some looks people ask for by name are not one filter but a few laid together, each on its own track, lowest first, over the same span. Those are the look packs (`emile/pink-phosphor`, `emile/receipt`, `emile/direct-flash`): read_timeline lists them, read_pack gives each one's guide (when, on what picture, what it can't do), and place_look lays its filters at once. Each filter clip stays its own and can be tuned or taken off afterwards.

## Looks that are made when the shot is made

These are not filters. They are how a shot is lit, what lens and camera it is taken with, how the camera moves: nothing laid over a finished shot can add them. Ask for them in the prompt when an image or a video is generated (or say they have to be shot that way), in these words; on a shot that already exists, say so and offer a newly generated shot instead.
